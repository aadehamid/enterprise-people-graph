Scope: Graph vs. Snowflake

Snowflake answers "how much / how many." The graph answers "how is it connected."
What stays in Snowflake (system of record):

- All metrics and aggregates (headcount, attrition, rating distributions, promotion rates)
- Full history and point-in-time snapshots
- Detailed attributes and raw text (full comments, feedback text)
- Dashboards, reporting and the existing semantic layer
- Governance, PII controls and certified data products

What goes into the graph:

- Entities: Employee (ID + key attributes), Manager / Org unit, Role / Job profile, Skill, Review cycle, Theme (extracted from check-ins, evaluations and feedback, not raw text), Promotion case, Talent Review placement
- Relationships:
    - Employee → reports to → Manager → rolls up to → Org
    - Employee → has skill → Skill
    - Employee → holds → Role; Role → requires → Skill
    - Employee → rated in → Review cycle (rating on the relationship)
    - Employee → gave / received feedback → Employee
    - Check-in / Evaluation / Feedback → mentions → Theme
    - Employee → nominated for → Promotion case (outcome on the relationship)
    - Employee → placed in → Talent Review category

How they connect:

- Graph is loaded from Snowflake on a schedule, keyed on Employee ID
- Graph holds only what's needed to traverse and filter; detailed records are looked up in Snowflake by ID
What the graph gives us that Snowflake can't easily do
- Similarity: employees with comparable skills, roles and trajectories (peer sets for calibration)
- Multi-hop paths: how ratings and feedback patterns move through a manager chain or org
- Skill adjacency: who is one or two skills away from a target role
- Evidence trails: themes, feedback, skills and outcomes connected to a rating or promotion decision, in one traversal
- Network signals: feedback connections across teams

3. Narrative Prose: Snowflake vs. Graph Architectural Boundary & Relationships

Architectural Boundary: Snowflake vs. People Graph
Snowflake as the System of Record:
Snowflake will remain the foundational system of record for all quantitative workforce metrics and aggregates, including headcount, attrition, rating distributions, and promotion rates. It will continue to store full historical records, point-in-time snapshots, detailed employee attributes, and unstructured raw text (such as full check-in comments and feedback narratives). All enterprise dashboards, standard reporting, the existing semantic layer, governance frameworks, PII controls, and certified data products will remain in Snowflake.
People Graph Scope (Entities & Relationships):
The Neo4j People Graph will be hydrated with a targeted subset of structured entities and relationships designed for multi-hop traversal and pattern analysis. Rather than duplicating raw text, the graph will ingest core Entities—specifically Employee (ID and key attributes), Manager / Org unit, Role / Job profile, Skill, Review cycle, Theme (structured topics extracted from check-ins, evaluations, and feedback), Promotion case, and Talent Review placement.
These entities are connected around the central Employee node through eight primary relationship patterns:

1. Organizational Hierarchy: Each Employee `reports to` a Manager, and that Manager `rolls up to` an Org (Organizational Unit), enabling multi-hop traversal across management chains and business units.
2. Employee Skills: An Employee is linked to specific Skill nodes via a `has skill` relationship, capturing their current capabilities.
3. Role & Skill Adjacency: An Employee `holds` a Role (Job Profile), and each Role `requires` specific Skill nodes. Combining this with the employee's existing skills allows the graph to calculate skill adjacency and identify who is one or two skills away from a target role.
4. Performance Ratings: An Employee is connected to a Review cycle via a `rated in` relationship, where the specific performance rating is stored directly as a property on the relationship edge.
5. Peer & Manager Feedback Network: Employee nodes are connected to other Employee nodes via `gave feedback` and `received feedback` relationships, mapping collaboration and feedback signals across teams.
6. Qualitative Themes: Rather than storing raw text in the graph, structured Check-in, Evaluation, and Feedback records are linked via a `mentions` relationship to extracted Theme nodes, creating navigable evidence trails.
7. Promotion History: An Employee is connected to a Promotion case via a `nominated for` relationship, with the final decision (e.g., approved or not approved) stored as a property on the relationship.
8. Talent Review Placement: An Employee is linked to a Talent Review category via a `placed in` relationship to capture their calibration and succession standing.
Structured Graph Schema Table
| Source Node                      | Relationship (Edge)               | Target Node            | Edge Properties / Notes                                          |
| -------------------------------- | --------------------------------- | ---------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Employee                         | REPORTS_TO                        | Manager                | Connects employee to direct supervisor                           |
| Manager                          | ROLLS_UP_TO                       | Org                    | Connects manager chain to organizational unit                    |
| Employee                         | HAS_SKILL                         | Skill                  | Captures provisioned or inferred employee skills                 |
| Employee                         | HOLDS                             | Role                   | Captures current or historical job profile                       |
| Role                             | REQUIRES                          | Skill                  | Defines target skills needed for a role                          |
| Employee                         | RATED_IN                          | Review cycle           | Stores the rating as a property on the relationship              |
| Employee                         | GAVE_FEEDBACK / RECEIVED_FEEDBACK | Employee               | Captures peer/manager feedback connections across teams          |
| Check-in / Evaluation / Feedback | MENTIONS                          | Theme                  | Links interaction records to extracted topics (no raw text)      |
| Employee                         | NOMINATED_FOR                     | Promotion case         | Stores the outcome (approved / not approved) on the relationship |
| Employee                         | PLACED_IN                         | Talent Review category | Tracks Talent Review placement                                   |

4. Glossary of Key Terms & Acronyms
- V2MOM (Vision, Values, Methods, Obstacles, Measures): Salesforce's strategic alignment and goal-setting framework captured in BaseCamp.
    - Vision: What you want to achieve.
    - Values: Principles and priorities guiding the work.
    - Methods: Actions and steps to get the job done.
    - Obstacles: Challenges to overcome.
    - Measures: Quantifiable metrics/milestones proving success.
    - Role in Graph: Represents "Employee Goals" and documented progress/gaps, used in Pilot 2 (Year-End Preparation) to identify discrepancies between documented progress and perceived performance.
- MYCI (Mid-Year Check-In): Formal mid-year performance and development conversation between an employee and manager captured in BaseCamp.
    - Role in Graph: Structured themes are extracted from MYCI comments (`Check-in → mentions → Theme`). Used as a signal filter in Pilot 1 (to verify if career growth was already discussed recently) and Pilot 3 (to identify newer hires past the April 16 cutoff who did not complete an optional MYCI).
- LPI (Low Performance Indicator): An initial performance flag or rating designation indicating an employee is not meeting performance expectations.
    - Role in Graph: In Pilot 4 (LPI Follow-Up), the graph checks whether an employee with an LPI has a connected follow-up action within an agreed number of days, prompting the manager if no action is documented.
- PIP (Performance Improvement Plan): A formal, structured timeline (typically 30–60 days) with measurable expectations to help an underperforming employee improve.
- PEP (Prompt Exit Package): A voluntary exit/severance option offered in the performance management workflow as an alternative to entering a formal PIP.
- ES / ESBP: Employee Success (HR) / Employee Success Business Partner.
- DGC: Data Governance Council (privacy and data governance review for sensitive workforce data).
- MCP (Model Context Protocol): The protocol used to expose relationship-aware graph tools alongside the existing Snowflake semantic layer under unified access controls and audit logging.
